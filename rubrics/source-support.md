# Source-support annotation rubric

Last updated: 2026-10-31

Annotate the supplied assertion at its stated time and scope. Preserve every
conjunction and qualification in a distinct span. A citation alone is not support.

- Supported: authorized, relevant, time-valid evidence entails the entire typed
  assertion with all qualifiers and consistent numeric assumptions.
- Contradicted: eligible evidence disagrees, or the numeric assertion fails. Keep
  both sides when sources conflict; do not choose the convenient source.
- Insufficient evidence: no eligible source, missing qualifier scope, excluded
  source, stale/future evidence, or unresolved evidence absence. A true assertion
  may have this label.
- Not fact-checkable: opinion outside the defined correctness event.

Separately label correctness against the synthetic generative facts, numeric
solutions and complete required claim set. A supported assertion may be false
when its source is wrong. Claim precision and answer correctness have different
denominators. Source trustworthiness is metadata, not a confidence probability.

The fixture creator checked the fifteen design cases against this rubric.
Independent human annotations remain pending. For a pilot and stratified final
audit, two independent annotators must see claims, frozen evidence and rubric,
without arm identity, predictions or raw confidence. Record both labels, relevant
qualifications, time burden, disagreement and adjudication with evidence. Include
all evidence states, numeric errors, each track and shifted domains. Never discard
difficult disagreements. Qualified engineering review is needed for real
engineering data; the current content is synthetic and provisional.

Audit packets must be exported before supplying annotations. Import only explicit
labels keyed by audit ID; reject missing/extra/duplicate IDs and unknown states.
Do not describe executable oracle checks or an agent's own review as human audit.
